# Lutnick ethics / recusal analysis — USA Rare Earth / Cantor

**Run:** `usa-rare-earth-cantor-depth-3-2026-08-09`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** active; factual and legal classification remains provisional pending Commerce ethics records

## Why this sub-question matters

The central ethics question is not merely whether Howard Lutnick still owned Cantor when Commerce negotiated with USA Rare Earth (USAR). Public records establish two separate restrictions with different timelines:

1. an ownership-based financial-conflict restriction under 18 U.S.C. § 208 while divestiture remained incomplete; and
2. a former-employer impartiality commitment under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502 covering particular matters involving specific parties in which Cantor was a party or represented a party for one year after Lutnick's resignation from Cantor.

The January 2026 USAR transaction occurred after the October 2025 divestiture closing but before the one-year former-employer period ended.

## Confirmed chronology

### February 18, 2025 — resignation from Cantor management

SEC filings state that Howard Lutnick stepped down from his executive and managerial positions with Cantor Fitzgerald L.P. and CF Group Management, Inc. effective February 18, 2025, after Senate confirmation as Commerce Secretary. Brandon Lutnick became Cantor chairman/CEO and CFGM CEO; Kyle Lutnick became Cantor executive vice chairman and CFGM president.

**Source:** SEC Schedule 13D amendment and later BGC filings.

### Ethics agreement — one-year former-employer restriction

The February 2026 congressional letter reproduces the relevant ethics-agreement commitment: under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502, Lutnick agreed not to participate personally and substantially in a particular matter involving specific parties in which Cantor Fitzgerald L.P. or a subsidiary was a party or represented a party for one year after resignation, or longer while an ownership interest continued.

Given the documented February 18, 2025 Cantor resignation, the one-year former-employer period ran through approximately February 18, 2026, absent a valid authorization or other controlling ethics determination.

**Analytic consequence:** the November 2025 USAR meeting, the approximately January 16, 2026 placement-agent engagement, and the January 26, 2026 Commerce/PIPE announcement all fall inside that one-year period.

### July 8, 2025 — limited White House § 208 waiver

The Counsel to the President issued a limited interim waiver under 18 U.S.C. § 208(b)(1) while Lutnick awaited regulatory approvals needed to complete his divestiture.

The waiver was **not a blanket Cantor-conflict waiver**. Its scope was particular matters of general applicability that could affect Cantor, BGC, or Newmark while the sale remained pending.

The waiver expressly excluded specific-party matters where Cantor, BGC, or Newmark was a party or represented a party. It also describes a Commerce ethics gatekeeping arrangement intended to screen Lutnick from specific-party matters and says the waiver did not authorize meetings with representatives of those firms.

**Source:** White House, `Howard W. Lutnick — Limited Waiver Pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208(b)(1)`, July 8, 2025.

### October 6, 2025 — Cantor divestiture closes

Multiple SEC filings state that Lutnick completed his divestiture of Cantor/CFGM interests on October 6, 2025. The voting shares of CFGM were sold to trusts controlled by Brandon Lutnick. SEC filings state that Howard Lutnick thereafter no longer had voting or dispositive power over the covered securities.

The White House waiver itself was time-limited to the period required to obtain approvals and execute the sales; once the assets were transferred, that interim § 208 waiver was no longer necessary by its own terms.

**Important distinction:** completion of the October divestiture ended the ownership-based problem addressed by the interim § 208 waiver. It did not, on the face of the documents reviewed, erase the separate one-year former-employer impartiality commitment that ran from the February 18 resignation.

### November 2025 — USAR / Lutnick meeting

Congressional investigators state that USAR CEO Barbara Humpton met Lutnick in early November 2025 and that Commerce engagement accelerated afterward. The underlying calendars, meeting notes, and ethics-screening records have not yet been located in the reviewed public record.

### On or about January 16, 2026 — Cantor/Moelis placement engagement

USAR's SEC-filed securities purchase agreement states that placement-agent engagement letters with Cantor and Moelis were dated on or about January 16, 2026.

### January 26, 2026 — Commerce LOI and Cantor-led PIPE announced

Commerce announced its tentative USAR financing package the same day USAR announced the $1.5 billion PIPE. Cantor was lead placement agent and Moelis co-placement agent. USAR's SEC materials state that raising at least $500 million from non-federal sources was part of the path to definitive federal financing documents.

## What the July waiver does — and does not — answer

### It answers

- Lutnick had a disclosed financial-conflict problem while divestiture was pending.
- The White House allowed him to work on broad matters of general applicability that could incidentally affect Cantor/BGC/Newmark.
- The waiver did not authorize specific-party matters where those firms were a party or represented a party.
- Commerce had a gatekeeping/screening arrangement for such specific-party matters.
- The waiver ended with completion of the divestiture process contemplated by the document.

### It does not answer

- whether Commerce ethics officials classified the USAR financing as a particular matter involving specific parties for § 2635.502 purposes;
- whether Cantor's role in the private PIPE meant it was a party to, or represented a party in, the same particular matter as the Commerce-USAR financing;
- whether the private PIPE and federal financing were treated as separate matters for ethics-screening purposes despite the capital condition linking them;
- whether Lutnick received a separate written authorization under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d);
- whether Lutnick was screened from any portion of the USAR matter;
- whether Lutnick personally and substantially participated in a covered matter after Cantor became involved;
- what Commerce ethics officials knew about Cantor's engagement and when they knew it.

## Current legal/evidentiary assessment

### Stronger than before

The previously identified ethics gap is now concrete rather than generic. The January 2026 transaction occurred within the documented one-year former-employer period, and the publicly released July 2025 waiver does not provide an obvious safe harbor for a specific-party matter involving Cantor.

### Still not enough to call a violation

A violation cannot be established from chronology alone. Two factual/legal predicates remain unresolved:

1. **same-matter / representation predicate:** whether Cantor was a party or represented a party in the relevant Commerce-USAR particular matter, rather than only in a legally distinct private PIPE; and
2. **participation / authorization predicate:** whether Lutnick participated personally and substantially in the covered matter without a valid § 2635.502(d) authorization or other ethics determination.

The public evidence reviewed so far supports **heightened conflict-of-interest scrutiny**, not a concluded ethics violation.

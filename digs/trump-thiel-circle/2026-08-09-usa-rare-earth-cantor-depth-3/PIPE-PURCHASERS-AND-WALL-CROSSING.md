# PIPE purchasers and preannouncement information access

**Run:** `usa-rare-earth-cantor-depth-3-2026-08-09`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** active research

## Question

Who bought the January 2026 $1.5 billion USA Rare Earth PIPE, what government-transaction information did those purchasers receive before public announcement, what trading restrictions applied, and is there public evidence that any purchaser traded USAR while aware of material non-public information?

## Bottom line

The SEC record establishes a conventional but important **wall-crossed private-placement process**:

1. USAR sold exactly **69,767,442 shares at $21.50**, producing an arithmetically exact subscription total of **$1,500,000,003** from the registered PIPE allocations.
2. The February 3 S-1 identifies the full purchaser/resale-registration allocation.
3. The Securities Purchase Agreement states that each purchaser relied on a **January 2026 Investor Presentation** most recently provided to it in connection with the investment and specifically acknowledged the non-binding U.S.-government Letter of Intent.
4. The filed Investor Presentation contained detailed proposed government economics, including the approximately **$1.6 billion** government investment structure, **$277 million** direct funding, **$1.3 billion** senior secured loan, government equity/warrant economics, and milestone/condition information.
5. USAR agreed to make a public **cleansing disclosure** containing the material terms and all company-provided material non-public information other than optional projected "Additional Information."
6. Each purchaser covenanted not to buy, sell, or short USAR securities from **execution of the purchase agreement until the transaction was publicly announced**.
7. The public announcement was released at **7:01 a.m. ET on January 26, 2026**; the associated SEC 8-K was accepted at **7:07:10 a.m. ET**.

These facts show that PIPE purchasers were deliberately brought inside a confidential transaction process and received government-transaction information before or as part of signing. **That is not evidence of insider trading.** Wall-crossing and cleansing are ordinary mechanisms used to permit confidential PIPE marketing while controlling securities-law risk.

The unresolved question is narrower and testable: **did any purchaser, related trading desk, affiliate, or person trade USAR before the public cleansing disclosure while aware of material non-public information obtained through the transaction process?** No such prohibited trading has been established in the reviewed public record.

## Related-party purchaser: Inflection Point Freedom Fund

The largest allocation was not merely an outside institutional investor.

USAR's S-1 discloses that:

- **Inflection Point Freedom Fund LP bought 13,955,000 shares for $300,032,500**.
- Michael Blitzer, chairman of USAR's board, is a member of the fund's three-person investment committee.
- Kevin Shannon, an adviser to USAR's board, is also on the fund's investment committee.
- USAR Chief Legal Officer and Secretary David Kronenfeld is a limited partner of the fund.
- The fund's general partner is entitled to a performance allocation and approximately **$1.3 million one-time set-up fee**.
- USAR states that the $21.50 price, other PIPE terms, and allocations were approved by a pricing committee consisting solely of disinterested directors following a customary private-placement process led by the placement agents.
- USAR states that Blitzer, Shannon, and the fund did not negotiate the PIPE transaction documents.
- USAR states that its audit committee approved the fund's participation and allocation under its related-party transaction policy before execution of the PIPE purchase agreement.

### Classification

This is a **confirmed, disclosed related-party transaction**. The disclosure itself supplies governance controls and counterevidence against an unsupported claim of hidden self-dealing. It remains relevant because the fund received the largest allocation in a financing directly linked to the government transaction.

## Other control / beneficial-ownership disclosures

The S-1 identifies institutional entities rather than claiming personal purchases by their founders or executives. The distinctions matter.

### Citadel CEMF — 4.19M shares / $90.085M

USAR states Citadel Advisors LLC manages Citadel CEMF; Kenneth Griffin owns a controlling interest in Citadel GP LLC and **may be deemed** to share voting/dispositive power. The filing expressly says this is not an admission of beneficial ownership beyond securities actually owned.

### Blackstone affiliates — 3.49M shares / $75.035M

The allocation is spread among Blackstone-managed vehicles. SEC beneficial-ownership language traces control through Blackstone entities to Blackstone Group Management and founder Stephen A. Schwarzman, with the usual disclaimer of beneficial ownership except for direct holdings.

### Point72 Associates — 3.49M shares / $75.035M

USAR states Point72 Asset Management maintains investment/voting power and that Steven A. Cohen controls relevant Point72 management entities and **may be deemed** to beneficially own the securities; the filing also records disclaimers.

These disclosures justify graph edges for **management/control or deemed beneficial ownership**, not claims that Griffin, Schwarzman, or Cohen personally purchased the shares.

## What purchasers were told

### Contractual acknowledgement of the government deal

Section 3.2(g) of the January 26 Securities Purchase Agreement applies to each purchaser. Each purchaser represents that it relied on:

- its own investigation;
- USAR's SEC reports;
- the **January 2026 Investor Presentation most recently provided to that purchaser**; and
- company representations in the agreement.

The same section expressly requires each purchaser to acknowledge that the **Letter of Intent was non-binding** and that completion of the Government Financing remained subject to definitive agreements.

That language is significant because the agreement separately defines Government Financing to include transactions involving the U.S. federal government and government equity/debt instruments.

### Investor Presentation contained detailed proposed government terms

The January Investor Presentation filed publicly at announcement includes, among other things:

- approximately $1.6B proposed U.S. government investment;
- $277M direct funding awards;
- $1.3B senior secured loan;
- proposed 15-year loan term and pricing assumptions;
- milestone-based disbursement;
- proposed government common-stock consideration;
- proposed warrants representing 10% of pre-PIPE fully diluted shares; and
- explicit description of the transaction as based on a non-binding government LOI.

Because the purchase agreement says purchasers relied on the presentation "as most recently provided" in connection with the investment and separately acknowledges the LOI, the evidence supports that the government transaction was part of the confidential investment process before public announcement.

## Cleansing mechanics

Section 4.5 required USAR, by the contractual Disclosure Time, to issue a press release and/or 8-K disclosing:

1. material terms of the PIPE; and
2. **all material non-public information supplied by USAR or its agents to purchasers**, except optional projected "Additional Information."

Upon that disclosure, the agreement states that purchasers who had not elected the Additional Information would no longer possess company-provided MNPI and their relevant confidentiality obligations would terminate.

Purchasers who elected to receive Additional Information remained subject to applicable law because USAR expressly did not promise to publicly cleanse those projections.

### Disclosure timing

The agreement defines Disclosure Time as no later than **9:01 a.m. New York time** on the relevant trading day depending on execution time.

USAR's public GlobeNewswire announcement is timestamped **7:01 a.m. ET on January 26, 2026**. The SEC filing was accepted at **7:07:10 a.m. ET**.

The exact timestamp at which each purchaser executed its agreement has not been located in the public exhibits.

## Trading restriction

Section 4.12 states that each purchaser and persons acting on its behalf or pursuant to an understanding with it could not execute purchases, sales, or short sales of USAR securities during the period:

`execution of purchase agreement -> first public announcement`

The agreement also required confidentiality until public disclosure.

After public disclosure, the contractual trading prohibition ended for purchasers who were cleansed, subject to applicable securities law. Purchasers retaining optional Additional Information remained subject to applicable-law restrictions.

### Multi-manager / information-barrier carveout

The same section expressly recognizes:

- multi-managed investment vehicles whose portfolio managers do not know other managers' investment decisions; and
- internal information barriers that wall off trading personnel.

For those investors, the contractual restriction applies to the relevant portfolio portion or walled-off personnel.

This is further evidence that the documents were drafted around a conventional wall-cross / information-barrier process.

## What is NOT established

The reviewed evidence does **not** establish that:

- any purchaser traded USAR while aware of prohibited MNPI;
- any purchaser received an improper leak rather than information through the documented PIPE process;
- any purchaser knew the government's proposed terms before it consented to confidentiality/wall-cross restrictions;
- a related trading desk breached an information barrier;
- Griffin, Schwarzman, Cohen, Blitzer, or another named individual personally traded USAR on the information;
- the $21.50 allocation price was improperly set;
- the related-party Inflection Point allocation was concealed; or
- the wall-cross process itself constitutes insider trading.

## Current classification

**Confirmed:** named institutional purchasers bought the PIPE; the complete allocation is publicly reconstructable; purchasers were given a January investor presentation and acknowledged the government LOI; the presentation contained detailed government economics; the transaction documents required confidentiality, a no-trade period from execution through public announcement, and cleansing of company-provided MNPI; the largest allocation was a disclosed USAR related-party transaction involving a fund whose investment committee included the board chairman.

**Unresolved:** when each investor first became aware of the government transaction, what pre-execution confidentiality restrictions applied, whether any relevant account traded USAR before cleansing, and whether any information barrier failed.

# Fee analysis — January 2026 USAR PIPE

**Run:** `usa-rare-earth-cantor-depth-3-2026-08-09`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Question:** How much did USA Rare Earth actually pay Cantor Fitzgerald for acting as lead placement agent on the January 2026 $1.5 billion PIPE?

## Bottom line

The public SEC record reviewed through August 9, 2026 does **not disclose Cantor's individual fee**.

What it does disclose is much tighter than the earlier public reporting:

- PIPE gross proceeds recorded by USAR: **$1.499993 billion** in the equity statement and $1.500 billion in the cash-flow statement.
- PIPE / securities-issuance costs recorded by USAR: **$51.003 million**.
- Implied aggregate issuance-cost burden: approximately **3.4002% of gross PIPE proceeds**.
- Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. and Moelis & Company LLC were the two defined "Placement Agents" in the SEC-filed Securities Purchase Agreement.
- USAR was solely responsible for the placement agents' fees, costs, expenses, and commissions.
- The economic terms owed to the placement agents were contained in separate engagement letters dated on or about **January 16, 2026**.
- Those engagement letters were **not filed as exhibits** with the January 26 8-K; the SEC filing index lists the purchase agreement, registration-rights agreement, press release, investor presentation, and government-transaction information, but not the engagement letters.

Therefore **$51.003 million must not be attributed to Cantor**. It is the aggregate securities-issuance / PIPE financing-cost figure and may include compensation to Cantor, compensation to Moelis, counsel and other issuance expenses.

## Primary accounting evidence

USAR's Form 10-Q for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 records:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| PIPE financing | $1,499.993M |
| PIPE financing costs | **$51.003M** |
| Cash-flow proceeds from common stock PIPE | $1,500.000M |
| Cash-flow payment of securities issuance costs | **$51.003M** |

The accounting presentation is unusually useful because it establishes a precise aggregate cost rather than the rounded "approximately $50 million" used in congressional correspondence.

### Derived metric

`$51.003M / $1,499.993M = ~3.4002%`

This **3.4002% is not a placement-agent rate**. It is only the aggregate issuance-cost ratio for the PIPE as recorded in USAR's accounts.

## Contract evidence

The January 26 Securities Purchase Agreement defines:

`Placement Agents = Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. + Moelis & Company LLC`

The agreement states that:

- USAR is solely responsible for payment of the placement agents' fees, costs, expenses, and commissions;
- compensation was paid to the placement agents in connection with placement of the securities; and
- duties/obligations under the separate placement-agent engagement letters survived the purchase agreement.

Section 5.3 identifies the relevant engagement letters as dated **on or about January 16, 2026**.

The public purchase agreement does **not** state the percentage split, cash fee, expense cap, tail fee, or other compensation terms.

## Filing-index check

The January 26, 2026 8-K filing contains 41 documents/data files. Its substantive exhibits include:

- certificate amendment;
- warrant amendment;
- pre-funded warrant form;
- Securities Purchase Agreement;
- Registration Rights Agreement;
- press release;
- investor presentation; and
- additional government-transaction information.

The Cantor/Moelis engagement letters are not listed as exhibits.

This explains why the purchase agreement can incorporate fee obligations by reference without exposing the amount publicly in that filing.

## What Congress said — correctly classified

The July 7 congressional inquiry notes that no public estimate of Cantor's actual fee had been released, cites broad PIPE-industry ranges, and points to approximately $50 million in USAR financing costs. The lawmakers then ask USAR and Cantor for:

- the negotiated Cantor rate;
- Cantor's dollar compensation;
- a breakdown of the ~$50M financing-cost total; and
- compensation paid to Moelis and other agents.

Those questions are consistent with the SEC evidence: the public filings establish the aggregate cost but not its allocation.

The letter's statement that Cantor may have received millions or tens of millions is an **estimate/allegation**, not a disclosed transaction fact.

## Search for post-deadline response

The July 7 letters requested responses by **July 23, 2026**. As of this research cutoff, searches of congressional releases, public web results, and indexed USAR/Cantor material did not locate a public response disclosing the fee breakdown.

This is a **negative public-source search result**, not proof that no response was sent privately or is held by congressional offices.

## Fee bounds: what can and cannot be said

### Hard upper bound from the public accounting record

Cantor's direct cash compensation attributable to the PIPE cannot rationally exceed the full **$51.003M** aggregate issuance-cost pool without some unrecorded/reclassified compensation mechanism. That is an accounting bound, not evidence that Cantor received anything close to the full amount.

### Lower bound

The purchase agreement establishes that compensation was payable to the placement agents, but it does not permit a reliable Cantor-specific dollar lower bound from the public text alone.

### No defensible split yet

Because Cantor was lead placement agent and Moelis was co-placement agent, role labels alone are insufficient to allocate the $51.003M. Legal expenses and other securities-issuance costs also need to be subtracted before any placement-agent fee pool can be determined.

## Comparison with other USAR transactions

Cantor had already served as lead placement agent on USAR's $75 million April-May 2025 PIPE. That precedent confirms an established issuer-agent relationship but does not supply the January 2026 fee rate unless the earlier engagement agreement or fee disclosure can be located and shown to govern or inform the later engagement.

Do **not** transplant a fee percentage from an unrelated PIPE or from generalized industry estimates into the January transaction.

## Highest-value remaining fee evidence

1. **Jan. 16, 2026 Cantor engagement letter** — primary target.
2. **Jan. 16, 2026 Moelis engagement letter** — needed to allocate the agent fee pool.
3. USAR general-ledger/accounting detail behind the $51.003M securities-issuance-cost entry.
4. Invoices, wire records, closing statement, funds-flow memorandum, or payment schedule from the Jan. 28 closing.
5. Board/finance-committee materials approving the placement-agent engagements and fees.
6. USAR response to Congress's July 7 letter, if released or obtainable.
7. Cantor response to Congress's July 7 letter, if released or obtainable.
8. Any later SEC filing that separately reclassifies or itemizes the $51.003M.

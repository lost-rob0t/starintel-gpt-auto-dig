# Commerce ELPO gatekeeping / recusal search

**Run:** `usa-rare-earth-cantor-depth-3-2026-08-09`  
**Research cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** active research; public-source search has not yet located a USAR-specific authorization or screening memorandum

## Objective

Determine whether the Department of Commerce Ethics Law and Programs Office (ELPO), the Office of Government Ethics (OGE), the White House, or another public source exposes the ethics-screening record governing Howard Lutnick's participation in the USA Rare Earth transaction while Cantor Fitzgerald was an existing USAR financial intermediary and Lutnick remained inside his one-year former-employer period.

The specific target records are:

- the Commerce ELPO gatekeeping agreement referenced by the July 8, 2025 White House waiver;
- any USAR-specific disqualification / recusal memorandum;
- any written authorization under 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d);
- any advice memorandum classifying whether the Commerce-USAR transaction and Cantor-led private financing were the same or separate "particular matter involving specific parties";
- screening-list entries for Cantor, its subsidiaries, USAR, Moelis, the PIPE, or the CHIPS transaction;
- calendar / meeting screening records; and
- communications between ELPO and the Commerce deal team concerning Lutnick's participation.

## Confirmed public ethics-program structure

### Commerce ELPO handles conflicts, disqualification and waiver questions

Commerce's official conflicts page states that conflicts based on financial interests or personal relationships can require an employee's **disqualification from certain matters**. It also states that, in some circumstances, an employee may obtain a **waiver** allowing participation that otherwise would be prohibited, and directs employees to the Ethics Law and Programs Office.

This makes ELPO the logical custodian/advisory office for the Lutnick/Cantor screening issue.

### Commerce ELPO administers public financial disclosure

Commerce's official financial-disclosure page states that ELPO administers the Department's OGE Form 278 public-financial-disclosure program as well as confidential OGE Form 450 reporting.

That is relevant because Lutnick's ethics agreement, compliance certification, divestiture reporting, and any advice based on disclosed interests sit within the same executive-branch ethics architecture.

## White House waiver creates a concrete gatekeeping-document target

The July 8, 2025 White House limited waiver under 18 U.S.C. § 208(b)(1) is already preserved in this research pass.

Its significance for this sub-pass is narrow:

- it addressed the period while Lutnick's divestitures awaited required approvals;
- it permitted participation in specified broad matters of general applicability despite covered financial interests;
- it did **not** provide a blanket authorization for specific-party matters involving Cantor/BGC/Newmark; and
- it described a Commerce ethics gatekeeping/screening arrangement intended to prevent Lutnick from participating in specific-party matters involving the covered companies.

Accordingly, the gatekeeping agreement is not hypothetical. The White House waiver itself points to a screening mechanism whose operational terms are relevant to the USAR chronology.

## Separate former-employer issue survives the October divestiture

Lutnick's covered Cantor divestiture was completed October 6, 2025. The interim ownership-based § 208 waiver therefore does not by itself resolve the January 2026 USAR matter.

The separate ethics-agreement commitment reproduced in official congressional correspondence states that Lutnick would, for one year after resignation from Cantor (or longer while ownership continued), not participate personally and substantially in a particular matter involving specific parties in which Cantor or a subsidiary was a party or represented a party, absent the applicable ethics-law mechanism.

Based on his February 18, 2025 resignation date, the following events fell inside that one-year period:

- the November 2025 meeting in which Lutnick says Ken Moelis introduced USAR executives to him;
- Lutnick's introduction of USAR to the CHIPS team;
- the on-or-about January 16, 2026 Cantor/Moelis placement-agent engagement;
- the January 26 Commerce LOI / PIPE announcement; and
- the January 28 PIPE closing.

This makes a USAR-specific ELPO classification or authorization especially probative.

## OGE public-document path

OGE's official public-resource pages state that its individual-disclosure search collection includes:

- ethics agreements; and
- **Certifications of Ethics Agreement Compliance** for Senate-confirmed presidential appointees.

OGE describes the certification as confirming compliance with commitments documented in the official's ethics agreement. OGE guidance also states that agencies submit these PAS compliance certifications to OGE.

Therefore, at minimum, a Lutnick Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance is a defined public-document type that should be searched/requested separately from an internal USAR recusal memorandum.

### Important distinction

A general Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance would establish that Lutnick and the ethics program certified completion/compliance with ethics-agreement commitments as of the certification process. It would **not necessarily answer** how a later, transaction-specific USAR/Cantor matter was screened in November 2025-January 2026.

The transaction-specific ELPO record remains necessary.

## Why the missing classification matters

The public evidence now establishes all of the following simultaneously:

1. Lutnick personally originated the government-side contact by introducing USAR to the CHIPS team after Ken Moelis introduced USAR to him.
2. This occurred during his one-year former-employer period for Cantor.
3. Cantor already had a pre-existing USAR placement-agent relationship.
4. Cantor became lead placement agent on the $1.5B PIPE that satisfied USAR's disclosed non-federal-capital condition for reaching definitive government financing.
5. The public White House waiver was not a blanket specific-party authorization involving Cantor and contemplated screening for such matters.

What remains missing is the ethics-office answer to the decisive classification questions:

- Did ELPO view the Commerce-USAR government financing as a particular matter involving specific parties?
- Did Cantor's role as USAR's placement agent mean Cantor was itself a party or was representing a party in that same matter?
- Did ELPO treat the federal financing and PIPE as legally/ethically separate matters despite their disclosed economic dependency?
- Was Lutnick screened from some transaction stages but permitted to make the initial introduction?
- Was a written § 2635.502(d) authorization issued?
- What did the ethics office know about Cantor's engagement, and when?

Until those questions are answered, chronology supports heightened scrutiny but not a concluded ethics violation.

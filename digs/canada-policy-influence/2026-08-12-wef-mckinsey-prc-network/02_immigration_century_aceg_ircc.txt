I. CENTURY INITIATIVE — HISTORICAL CORE

2016
- Century Initiative publicly advocated a long-term Canadian population of 100 million by 2100, with higher immigration as a central mechanism. [A/HIGH]
- A 2016 launch/panel record includes Dominic Barton, Goldy Hyder, Kate Subak, and Mark Wiseman. [A/HIGH]

2019
- Century Initiative stated that it was led by founders and board directors:
  * Mark Wiseman
  * Dominic Barton
  * Willa Black
  * Goldy Hyder
  * Andrew Pickersgill
- Its “Bigger, Bolder Canada” work called for immediate increases in immigration as part of a larger population-growth strategy. [A/HIGH]

2018 / networking event
- Century Initiative’s “Path to 100 Million” event placed Dominic Barton on a panel with then-Immigration Minister Ahmed Hussen, Goldy Hyder, and Sevaun Palvetzian, with special remarks by Mark Wiseman. [A/HIGH for event/network edge]
- This proves contact/convening, not policy causation.

2019 immigrant-integration collaboration
- Century Initiative held an immigrant-integration expert roundtable in Montreal in partnership with RBC.
- Around 50 attendees came from academia, business, think tanks, and nonprofits.
- The event featured a presentation of the “CI-McKinsey Achieving Better Integration Outcomes” report.
- This is a direct Century Initiative ↔ McKinsey organizational collaboration on immigration integration. [A/HIGH]

McKinsey-origin personnel link
- McKinsey’s own career profile for “Aleema” says she originally joined McKinsey as a Toronto Business Analyst, took roughly five years away “to start a Canadian non-profit called the Century Initiative,” attended Harvard Law School, and later returned to McKinsey as an engagement manager. [A/HIGH]
- This is one of the strongest direct personnel links found between McKinsey and Century Initiative.

III. ADVISORY COUNCIL ON ECONOMIC GROWTH (ACEG)

Formation
- Federal Finance Minister established ACEG in 2016 as an external advisory council for concrete policy actions to improve Canadian economic growth. [A/HIGH]
- Dominic Barton chaired the Council.

McKinsey support
- House of Commons committee evidence and report document that McKinsey provided a pro-bono secretariat/research team.
- Andrew Pickersgill said the Council approached McKinsey and he helped lead the team and coordinate expertise from around the world.
- Witnesses Barton, Pickersgill, and Michael Sabia described the McKinsey team as primarily research support and testified that McKinsey employees did not make the Council’s recommendations.
- The team was paid by McKinsey, at no charge to the Council. [A/B/HIGH]

Immigration recommendations
ACEG recommended:
1. Gradually increase annual permanent immigration to 450,000 over five years.
2. Facilitate entry for senior/specialized talent.
3. Qualify more international students for permanent residence.
4. Improve national accreditation standards for skilled immigrants.

The detailed ramp proposed:
- 2017: 315,000
- 2018: 330,000
- 2019: 360,000
- 2020: 405,000
- 2021: 450,000

The proposal sought a net increase rather than offsetting the increase through reductions in family/refugee/humanitarian streams. [A/HIGH]

ACEG-to-implementation evidence
ACEG’s December 2017 “Ideas into Action” report says it:
- worked iteratively with stakeholders and the public sector;
- worked interactively with Finance Canada and other departments to build support and prepare the ground for implementation.

It then stated that the government had taken action on specific immigration recommendations:
- Express Entry points reforms;
- Global Skills Strategy, launched June 2017, with a two-week standard for certain visas/work permits;
- Targeted Employment Strategy for Newcomers.

The same report said the 2018–2020 government immigration plan, rising to 340,000 by 2020, was below ACEG’s 450,000 recommendation but a “step in the right direction.” [A/HIGH]

Causality limit
- This is direct evidence of an advisory body identifying its own recommendations and subsequent policy implementation.
- It does NOT establish that McKinsey as a corporation ordered or secretly chose immigration levels.
- The strongest safe statement is: McKinsey supplied research/secretariat support to an advisory council chaired by a McKinsey leader; the council recommended major immigration changes; the council’s own report later attributed several government actions to its recommendations.

IV. DIRECT IRCC ↔ MCKINSEY ADMINISTRATIVE TRANSFORMATION

IRCC official record says:
- two competitive McKinsey contracts between 2018 and 2020/2021;
- combined contract value: $27.7 million;
- first: $2.9 million after amendments;
- second: $24.8 million.

First contract work
McKinsey:
- performed diagnostic assessment;
- conducted site visits;
- interviewed department leaders;
- analyzed service delivery and transformation plans;
- developed a service-transformation strategy;
- produced a step-by-step roadmap;
- produced two operating models: one for process efficiency and another for digital service delivery.

IRCC then:
- established transformation teams;
- established a Chief Transformation Officer function;
- pursued process improvement and digital-first/user-centered operating models.

Second contract work included guidance/input on:
- Delhi-office transformation pilot;
- new operating models for faster user-centric technology delivery;
- digital services/tools for visas, study permits, citizenship, and some permanent-residence classes;
- recruiting practices for digital talent;
- transformation office / digital strategy / roadmap work.

IRCC explicitly says the work contributed to its ability to achieve unprecedented permanent-residence processing levels, although that does not mean McKinsey set the policy quotas. [A/HIGH]

VI. OFFICIAL COUNTER-EVIDENCE / LIMITS

- IRCC officials stated that, to their knowledge, McKinsey did not advise IRCC on immigration thresholds.
- Minister Sean Fraser rejected the claim that McKinsey made federal immigration-level policy decisions; he characterized McKinsey’s IRCC work as service/digital transformation.
- Some McKinsey work touched processing systems, operating models, digital transformation, and a Phase 3 business case; those are implementation/administration edges, not proof of quota-setting authority.

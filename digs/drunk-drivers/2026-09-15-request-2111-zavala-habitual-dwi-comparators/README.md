# Request #2111 — Texas habitual-DWI comparator baseline

## Question

How does Eugenio Les Zavala's reported 50-year sentence compare with published Texas appellate cases involving felony DWI and repeat/habitual punishment enhancement?

## Evidence added

This pass adds four published appellate-opinion comparators:

- **Mueller v. State**, No. 04-20-00356-CR (Tex. App.—San Antonio June 15, 2022): felony DWI as a habitual offender; **50 years**; affirmed as modified.
- **Sutton v. State**, No. 03-23-00357-CR (Tex. App.—Austin Nov. 8, 2024): felony DWI with punishment-enhancement allegations found true; **50 years**; affirmed.
- **Villnave v. State**, No. 05-13-00617-CR (Tex. App.—Dallas Feb. 28, 2014): felony DWI with two prior felony punishment enhancements found true; **50 years**; affirmed.
- **Green v. State**, No. 11-03-00083-CR (Tex. App.—Eastland June 24, 2004): felony DWI with habitual-offender enhancement allegations found true; **25 years plus a fine**; affirmed.

The opinions were reviewed through public Justia reproductions of the appellate opinions. They are used as court-record comparators, not as evidence about Zavala's own predicates.

## Bounded conclusion

The located appellate record is enough to reject a claim that a **50-year Texas habitual/repeat-felony DWI sentence is unique or unprecedented**. Multiple published appellate cases document the same 50-year outcome.

It is **not** enough to say that 50 years is statistically typical, unusually severe, or part of a Montgomery County-specific pattern. Published appeals are a selected subset and this pass does not build a denominator-backed sentencing distribution. A 25-year comparator also demonstrates meaningful variation within the enhanced range.

# Beth Din civil-review ledger — issue #2646, Maryland pass 3

Worker 6/8 bounded continuation of the merged Beth Din evidence-state ledger. This pass expands the corpus beyond New York with an official Maryland appellate opinion. It is **not** a completion claim for issue #2646.

## Primary record

The Appellate Court of Maryland filed the unreported opinion **Yaakov Ezra Vogel v. Benjamin Moshe Vogel, Personal Representative, et al.**, No. 2119, September Term 2023, on August 12, 2025. The court identifies the rabbinical tribunal in the underlying record as the **Rabbinical Court of Cong. Agudath Israel of Los Angeles**, also described in the record as a Beth Din or Beis Din.

The underlying dispute concerned an estate and arbitration agreements signed by only some of the relevant people. A protective petition alleged, among other things, that the Beth Din refused to hear material evidence, engaged in misconduct prejudicing the Estate, exceeded its powers, and acted on matters not submitted to it. Those allegations are preserved as **allegations**, not findings.

The circuit court instead resolved the enforceability dispute on contract/consent grounds: not all necessary heirs were parties to the arbitration agreement, so the agreement was held null and void; the petition to confirm was denied and the protective petition to vacate/modify was dismissed without prejudice. The Appellate Court affirmed. Its disposition therefore does **not** adjudicate the pleaded misconduct allegations as true.

The opinion also records that a later religious appeal before an unnamed Beth Din in Israel concluded that the parties were not bound because not all inheritors had signed, while the Los Angeles Beth Din rejected that ruling. This pass does not invent an identity for the Israeli tribunal or treat the religious appeal as a Maryland judicial finding.

## Evidence-state classification

- Exact tribunal identity: public court-record observation only.
- Misconduct/refusal-to-hear allegations: pleaded, not adjudicated on the merits in the disposition preserved here.
- Judicial holding: arbitration agreement unenforceable/null and void as to the attempted binding effect because necessary non-signatories had not agreed to arbitrate.
- Appellate disposition: judgments affirmed.
- Corruption classification: **none established by this opinion**.

This is useful to #2646 because it adds a cross-jurisdiction example where serious allegations appear in the record but the judicial outcome rests on a different, narrower contract-consent ground. That distinction prevents allegation laundering.

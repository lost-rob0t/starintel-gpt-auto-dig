# Beth Din corruption / religious court abuse — deep Auto-Dig

Go hard on the records. No race angle, no collective blame, no mush.

## Task-scoped analyst

This packet runs with [`ANALYST.md`](./ANALYST.md), a **one-shot OSINT analyst for this dig only**.

It has no issue dependency and does not alter the global Auto-Dig persona. It stays locked to this packet until the completion condition is met, then exits and returns to normal/default Auto-Dig work.

Public forum/social/community posts can be captured when materially useful: record the source, tag the claim `unverified lead`, turn it into searches, and move on. Do not burn half the run arguing with the post.

## Mission

Enumerate and verify documented corruption, abuse, coercion, conflicts of interest, financial misconduct, procedural misconduct, retaliation, cover-ups, and accountability failures involving **Beth Din / batei din**.

Beth Din is the primary scope. Other religious courts are a secondary comparison pass only after the Beth Din corpus is mature enough to stand on its own.

This is an institutional corruption dig. Do not treat Jews, Jewish communities, rabbis, or religious people as a class as the target. The target is specific courts, actors, proceedings, money flows, governance structures, complaints, sanctions, litigation, and documented conduct.

## Enumerate

Build a normalized ledger of:

- Beth Din / tribunal names and aliases;
- jurisdiction and location;
- denomination or institutional affiliation where relevant;
- judges/dayanim;
- administrators and staff;
- attorneys and repeat representatives;
- parties to significant proceedings;
- intermediaries, fixers, advocates, communal organizations, schools, synagogues, charities, businesses, or employers materially involved in documented events;
- parent organizations and funding/governance relationships;
- civil courts reviewing or enforcing tribunal decisions;
- prosecutors, regulators, disciplinary bodies, and appellate courts involved.

For people, collect only public-interest information materially relevant to the case. No doxxing, no unrelated home/family data.

## Misconduct classes

Prioritize evidence of:

- bribery, kickbacks, improper payments, undisclosed fees, or self-dealing;
- undisclosed conflicts of interest, repeat-player favoritism, or insider relationships;
- coercion, threats, retaliation, blacklisting, social pressure, or extortionate conduct;
- manipulation or abuse of get/divorce proceedings;
- ex parte contacts, sham hearings, denial of notice, inability to present evidence, inconsistent procedure, or recusal failures;
- fabricated, altered, concealed, or selectively presented evidence;
- pressure on witnesses, complainants, litigants, counsel, rabbis, employers, schools, synagogues, or community institutions;
- cover-ups, suppression of complaints, whistleblower intimidation, or retaliation against critics;
- criminal conduct by judges/dayanim, administrators, associated fixers, or intermediaries;
- civil judgments, arbitration-vacatur decisions, sanctions, contempt findings, injunctions, disciplinary actions, or appellate criticism tied to tribunal conduct;
- repeated independent allegations against the same institution or actor;
- governance failures: opaque appointments, no meaningful appeal, financial opacity, weak recusal rules, unpublished procedure, or missing complaint mechanisms.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Court opinions, dockets, filings, judgments, injunctions, sanctions, vacatur decisions, and appellate opinions.
2. Indictments, plea agreements, convictions, sentencing records, law-enforcement releases, and regulator records.
3. Bar, judicial, rabbinical, professional, charity, corporate, and nonprofit disciplinary or registry records.
4. Official Beth Din rules, arbitration agreements, fee schedules, recusal rules, governance documents, published decisions, and archived versions.
5. Sworn declarations, deposition excerpts, exhibits, contemporaneous correspondence, authenticated recordings, and documentary evidence.
6. High-quality investigative reporting with named sources and primary-document links.
7. First-person allegations and community reporting as leads only until independently corroborated.

## Claim status

Every substantive claim must be tagged as exactly one of:

- `proven/adjudicated` — conviction, judgment, sanction, disciplinary finding, or equivalent authoritative result;
- `documented` — strongly supported by primary records but not finally adjudicated;
- `alleged` — claimed by a party/source and not independently established;
- `disputed` — materially contested by credible evidence or a named opposing party;
- `unverified lead` — worth further digging but presently unsupported.

Do not mutate allegations into facts because the allegation sounds nasty.

## Case ledger

For every meaningful event record:

- stable case ID;
- tribunal/institution;
- jurisdiction/location;
- people and organizations involved;
- date range;
- proceeding type;
- misconduct category;
- precise factual claim;
- claim status;
- strongest primary source;
- supporting sources;
- response/denial from accused party if available;
- court or disciplinary outcome;
- sanctions/remedies;
- financial amounts where documented;
- related cases/events;
- confidence score;
- unanswered questions.

## Pattern tests

Once the case ledger exists, test for real patterns:

- recurring actors across nominally separate tribunals;
- repeat litigants or counsel receiving unusual treatment;
- recurring conflicts or undisclosed ties;
- unusual fee flows;
- clusters of vacated arbitration awards;
- repeated coercion or retaliation allegations from independent complainants;
- procedural rules applied inconsistently;
- institutions lacking meaningful recusal, appeal, or complaint mechanisms;
- civil courts repeatedly criticizing the same tribunal or people;
- organizations steering disputes toward tribunals they influence financially or institutionally;
- chronology showing complaints preceding later proven misconduct.

One ugly case is not a system. Make the evidence earn the conclusion.

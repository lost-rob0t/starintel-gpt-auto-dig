## Operating definition

This pass does **not** treat every pro-Israel, Jewish, conservative, Republican, evangelical, or settlement-adjacent actor as part of one network.

An entity enters the core graph only when a reviewed source supports at least one of these functions:

1. advocates Israeli sovereignty, annexation, permanent control, or settlement expansion in Judea and Samaria / the West Bank;
2. administers, builds, finances, defends, or materially supports that territorial program;
3. provides a documented U.S. political, educational, lobbying, travel, fundraising, or tax-exempt bridge for such activity.

Association alone is not control. A donation channel alone does not prove that every dollar reached a named Israeli project. A meeting or trip alone does not prove that it caused a later vote.

## Executive assessment

The strongest person-first structure found in this pass is:

```text
Israeli municipal and settlement leadership
    -> Israeli umbrella / implementation organization
    -> U.S. nonprofit, delegation, testimony, conference, or legislative bridge
    -> U.S. political and donor audiences
```

The highest-confidence bridges are:

1. **Yesha Council -> American Friends of Judea and Samaria (AFJS).** The Yesha Council publicly links AFJS, while AFJS identifies itself as a U.S. 501(c)(3) founded in 2022 and says it supports awareness, housing, factories, agricultural infrastructure, and a one-million-resident vision.
2. **Regavim -> The Regavim Policy Forum.** Regavim's own donation page identifies a U.S. 501(c)(3), EIN `41-2923371`, and names The Regavim Policy Forum as the U.S. bank/check recipient in Woodmere, New York.
3. **Yesha leadership -> U.S. Republican and evangelical outreach.** Reuters documented Yisrael Ganz, Ruth Lieberman, Eliana Passentin, Ohad Tal, Pesach Wolicki, and related delegations engaging Mike Johnson, Mike Lee, Heritage Foundation events, the U.S. Israel Education Association, and other U.S. conservative or evangelical actors.
4. **Sovereignty advocacy -> Congress.** H.R. 902 and S. 384 sought to require U.S. government use of “Judea and Samaria.” A December 2025 House Foreign Affairs subcommittee hearing included written testimony by Zionist Organization of America president Morton Klein arguing for Israeli sovereignty and citing an AFJS map and Regavim materials.

The current record supports a connected advocacy and support ecosystem. It does **not** support a claim that one organization centrally commands every municipal council, Israeli government action, U.S. legislator, donor, or recipient organization.

**Analytic confidence:** `0.91`

## Layer 4 — U.S. cross-tie mechanisms

### A. Tax-exempt fundraising

```text
Yesha Council --publicly links--> AFJS --501(c)(3) fundraising/support--> Judea and Samaria projects
Regavim --names--> The Regavim Policy Forum --501(c)(3) donations--> Regavim research/policy/legal work
U.S. donor organizations --grants--> Israeli recipients [recipient-level schedules still required]
```

Strongly supported:

- AFJS identifies itself as a U.S. 501(c)(3), founded in 2022.
- AFJS says donations and membership support housing, factories, agricultural and humanitarian infrastructure, public education, and a one-million-resident vision.
- Regavim identifies its U.S. EIN and The Regavim Policy Forum as its U.S. receiving vehicle.

Not yet established:

- that every AFJS-funded project is selected or controlled by the Yesha Council;
- current AFJS and Regavim Policy Forum officer, contractor, and grant schedules;
- a direct current payment path from Central Fund of Israel, One Israel Fund, Hebron Fund, or American Friends of Ateret Cohanim to every Israeli organization named in this packet.

### B. Delegation and travel network

Reuters documented:

```text
Ruth Lieberman / USIEA
    -> privately financed trips by members of Congress
    -> Gush Etzion and other settlement visits

Yisrael Ganz + Ruth Lieberman + Eliana Passentin
    -> Washington delegation
    -> Speaker Mike Johnson and other U.S. officials
```

Trip records, ethics disclosures, itineraries, sponsors, briefers, and subsequent votes must be normalized before drawing causal conclusions.

### C. U.S. religious-conservative advocacy

```text
Ohad Tal + Pesach Wolicki + Tony Perkins
    -> Keep God's Land
    -> Heritage Foundation event and religious media
    -> Republican / evangelical audiences
```

Reuters specifically attributed to Tal a goal of strengthening evangelical connections and described the coalition's effort to influence Trump and the Republican Party against a two-state solution.

### D. Legislation and official terminology

- **H.R. 902** — introduced by Representative Claudia Tenney on January 31, 2025.
- **S. 384** — introduced by Senator Tom Cotton on February 4, 2025.
- Both sought official U.S. use of “Judea and Samaria” instead of “West Bank.”

The existence of legislation establishes advocacy and legislative action. It does not by itself prove that a specific Israeli organization drafted the text or controlled the sponsors.

### E. Congressional testimony and source laundering check

Morton Klein's December 2025 House testimony:

- openly advocated Israeli sovereignty;
- used an AFJS map;
- cited Regavim materials;
- was assisted by ZOA researcher Elizabeth Berney.

This is a documented U.S. amplification chain:

```text
AFJS / Regavim material
    -> ZOA research and testimony
    -> House Foreign Affairs subcommittee record
```

“Amplification” is supported. “Coordination” beyond source use is not established without communications, drafting records, or joint-event evidence.

## Evidence boundaries

### Supported

- public leadership and board roles listed by the organizations themselves;
- public organizational objectives and activities;
- U.S. tax-exempt channels explicitly named by AFJS and Regavim;
- documented U.S. meetings, visits, events, legislative actions, and testimony;
- attributed sanctions determinations by U.S. or EU authorities.

### Not supported without further records

- private membership lists;
- undocumented donor identities;
- causal control of votes or executive decisions;
- central command across independent organizations;
- criminal liability inferred solely from sanctions, advocacy, or association;
- assigning an organization-level U.S. tie to every employee or board member as a personal tie.

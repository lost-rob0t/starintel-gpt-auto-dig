# Greater Israel follow-on: WEF, Palantir funding, and AIPAC electoral finance

**Run date:** 2026-07-31  
**Parent run:** `greater-israel-people-orgs-us-ties-2026-07-31`  
**Scope:** Public organizational affiliations, corporate revenue, government awards, defense partnerships, PAC receipts, direct committee spending, independent expenditures, and documented election interventions.

## Assessment

The reviewed record supports three distinct networks that overlap at some people and institutions but should not be collapsed into one command structure:

1. **WEF access and convening:** Palantir is a World Economic Forum partner and Alex Karp is a recurring WEF participant. Benjamin Netanyahu has also repeatedly participated in WEF meetings and virtual programs.
2. **Palantir defense funding and operations:** Palantir receives substantial U.S. government revenue and major Defense Department awards, and separately disclosed a strategic technology partnership with the Israeli Defense Ministry.
3. **AIPAC electoral and lobbying influence:** AIPAC PAC, AIPAC's member donation portal, United Democracy Project, lobbying, and member mobilization form separate but complementary influence mechanisms.

The reviewed sources do **not** establish that WEF directs Israeli territorial policy, that Palantir finances settlement organizations, that U.S. Palantir contract dollars were earmarked for Israel, or that campaign spending proves control of an elected official.

## 1. WEF links

### Palantir and Alex Karp

The official WEF record establishes a direct institutional relationship:

- Palantir Technologies appears on the WEF partner list for the 2023 Annual Meeting of the New Champions.
- Palantir appears on the partner lists for both the 2026 World Economic Forum Annual Meeting and the 2026 Annual Meeting of the New Champions.
- Alex Karp participated in a WEF Annual Meeting conversation in 2023 and another WEF conversation on January 20, 2026.
- WEF's 2026 participant material listed Karp among prominent technology executives attending Davos.

This supports:

```text
Palantir Technologies --partner_of / participates_in--> World Economic Forum
Alex Karp --speaker_at / participant_in--> World Economic Forum meetings
```

It does not by itself establish that WEF controls Palantir contracts, customers, defense deployments, or political positions.

A separate Bloomberg report documented a Palantir-hosted Israel discussion in Davos in January 2024 attended by senior technology executives. That is a **Davos ecosystem event** and should not be coded as an official WEF session without additional WEF program evidence.

### Benjamin Netanyahu

Official WEF pages document Netanyahu's participation across multiple years and formats:

- a special address at the 2014 Annual Meeting;
- participation listed for the 2018 Annual Meeting;
- a May 2020 WEF COVID Action Platform meeting;
- an October 2020 Great Reset session on Fourth Industrial Revolution technology.

This supports repeated access and participation. It does not establish a WEF role in Netanyahu's coalition politics, settlement policy, military decisions, or territorial administration.

### Settlement-implementation organizations

## 2. Palantir funding and Israeli-defense links

### Corporate revenue base

Palantir's 2025 Form 10-K reported:

| Measure | FY2025 |
|---|---:|
| Total revenue | $4.475446 billion |
| Government revenue | $2.402287 billion |
| Government share of revenue | 54% |
| U.S. government revenue | $1.9 billion |
| Revenue from U.S. customers, all segments | 74% |

This establishes that U.S. public-sector purchasing is a major component of Palantir's corporate revenue base.

### Selected U.S. defense awards

Official Defense Department contract notices record:

- a May 2024 $480 million firm-fixed-price contract for the Maven Smart System prototype, contract `W911QX24-D-0012`;
- a September 2024 $99.804561 million contract for Maven Smart System licenses, software support, and hardware, contract `W911QX-24-D-0026`;
- a May 2025 $795 million modification to `W911QX-24-D-0012` for Maven Smart System software licenses.

These are award actions, ceilings, or modifications. They should not be blindly added as if each amount were a separate amount already obligated or paid. Work locations and funding are determined through orders under the contracts.

### Israeli Defense Ministry partnership

Palantir's SEC filings state that in 2024 the company agreed to a strategic partnership with the Israeli Defense Ministry to supply technology to Israel to assist in the ongoing war. The filings also state that Palantir's operations related to Israel and Ukraine were not material to its financial position or operating results at the time of those disclosures.

Bloomberg separately reported that Palantir was supplying Israel with new tools after October 7, 2023, that the company held a board meeting in Tel Aviv, and that Alex Karp and Peter Thiel met Israel's president.

Palantir's 2026 proxy materials deny recurring claims that it developed automated targeting systems allegedly used by the IDF in Gaza, stating that those systems were independent of or predated its Ministry of Defense partnership.

The supported graph is therefore:

```text
U.S. government customers --revenue / contract awards--> Palantir
Palantir --strategic technology partnership--> Israeli Defense Ministry
Palantir --partner / participant--> World Economic Forum
```

The following stronger graph is **not established**:

```text
U.S. Palantir contract dollars --earmarked for--> Israeli war operations or West Bank settlement activity
WEF --directs--> Palantir's Israeli Defense Ministry work
Palantir --funds--> Yesha Council, Amana, Regavim, Nachala, or settlement construction
```

### Palantir PAC

The FEC identifies Employees of Palantir Technologies Inc. PAC as a qualified corporate PAC connected to Palantir. For January 1, 2025 through May 31, 2026, the FEC summary reported:

- $79,094.63 in total receipts;
- $92,737.32 in total disbursements;
- $81,500 in contributions to other committees;
- no independent expenditures.

This is a real U.S. political-finance edge, but it is small relative to Palantir's government revenue and major defense awards. Recipient-level reconciliation remains a separate target.

## 3. AIPAC influence through donations and outside spending

### Stated political objective

AIPAC's own political page states that its members seek to keep Congress pro-Israel, help pro-Israel candidates win, and defeat detractors of the U.S.-Israel relationship. It describes:

- AIPAC PAC as supporting Democratic and Republican congressional candidates;
- individual member donations through the AIPAC Political Portal;
- United Democracy Project as an AIPAC-backed super PAC used to defeat targeted candidates.

AIPAC's public materials also connect electoral activity to policy goals including security assistance, missile-defense cooperation, and other U.S.-Israel programs.

### 2025–2026 FEC capacity

FEC summaries covering January 1, 2025 through May 31, 2026 reported:

| Committee | Receipts | Total disbursements | Contributions to other committees | Independent expenditures | Ending cash |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AIPAC PAC | $43,951,812.50 | $42,208,563.07 | Not separately displayed in the retrieved summary | $0 reported on the retrieved overview | $2,770,856.21 |
| United Democracy Project | $98,628,508.53 | $34,111,913.00 | $7,081,200.00 | $12,649,893.29 | $93,319,490.63 |

The legal and analytical distinction matters:

- **Direct PAC contributions** are money given to candidate or political committees within contribution limits and reporting rules.
- **Independent expenditures** pay for communications supporting or opposing candidates and are not direct payments to the candidates.
- **Political Portal donations** are individual contributions facilitated or promoted through AIPAC's member infrastructure.
- **Lobbying and member contacts** operate separately from election spending.

### Michigan Senate primary case

Associated Press reporting in July 2026 found that AIPAC and affiliated groups had spent close to $30 million backing Haley Stevens against Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, describing it as their largest single-race investment. AP reported that the spending materially reshaped the race and that Stevens largely avoided discussing AIPAC directly.

This is strong evidence of electoral intervention and agenda-setting capacity. It does not prove that AIPAC controls Stevens, that the spending alone caused voter decisions, or that every outside group supporting Stevens was AIPAC-affiliated.

### Influence model supported by the evidence

```text
AIPAC members / donors
  -> individual candidate contributions and AIPAC PAC receipts
  -> candidate support network

AIPAC-backed UDP donors
  -> super-PAC treasury
  -> independent advertising supporting or opposing candidates

AIPAC staff and members
  -> lobbying, alerts, congressional contacts, and policy advocacy

combined effect
  -> candidate incentives, election risk, access, message amplification,
     and demonstrated capacity to intervene in selected races
```

The record supports describing this as **organized political influence**. The record does not support replacing that formulation with an unqualified claim that AIPAC controls Congress or commands every recipient's votes.

## Evidence boundaries

- WEF partnership or event participation establishes affiliation, access, or convening—not operational control.
- A government contract amount may be a ceiling or award action rather than money already paid.
- Corporate revenue is fungible at the company level, but that does not prove a named public contract funded a separate foreign engagement.
- A strategic partnership establishes a relationship; product use, targeting decisions, and operational effects require separate evidence.
- A PAC contribution, bundled individual donation, endorsement, or independent expenditure establishes political support—not control, bribery, coordination, or vote causation.
- Absence from reviewed searches is an unresolved result, not proof of nonexistence.

## Question

Does the public record expose mechanisms through which Peter Thiel or a Thiel-aligned network could covertly direct, constrain, reward, or operationalize JD Vance?

## Current assessment

The hypothesis can no longer be reduced to “Thiel donated money and Vance shares his politics.”

The reviewed record establishes four concrete **covert-capable mechanisms**:

1. a donor-driven PAC built a proposed two-way campaign-data environment through a common vendor;
2. FEC counsel considered whether the arrangement could have been concealed through a subvendor and declined deeper investigation on prosecutorial-resource grounds;
3. Vance remains financially entangled with Narya through fund interests, carried interest, fee receivables, and a promissory note payable through 2028;
4. Rockbridge uses legally separate nonprofits, a super PAC, a private donor network, and a for-profit donor-management company to turn undisclosed capital into media, polling, turnout, church organizing, personnel, and access.

These facts support a **distributed covert-influence and dependency architecture**. They do not yet prove that Thiel is the sole controller, that Vance accepts direct orders, or that coercion, blackmail, or foreign direction occurred.

The strongest live model is layered:

`capital and private relationships`
→ `PAC and nonprofit intermediaries`
→ `data, polling, media, turnout, personnel, and access`
→ `candidate and administration decisions`
→ `policy, contracts, regulation, and portfolio benefits`
→ `renewed capital and institutional power`

## Mechanism 1 — donor tasking and shared campaign data

Protect Ohio Values PAC's September 28, 2021 confidential memorandum said the PAC had hired Tony Fabrizio, Prospect Strategic, and Deep Root Analytics to build research, models, segments, and issue-message audiences around Vance.

The memo described an intended two-way arrangement:

- the Vance campaign would access voter universes modeled and paid for by the PAC;
- the PAC would access voter-contact data uploaded by the campaign;
- the arrangement would give Vance's campaign presidential-level targeting without requiring it to pay the audience-construction cost.

The FEC record states that the memo was posted after donor pressure to “do more to support Vance immediately.” This documents a mechanism through which donor priorities could be translated into target audiences, campaign messages, polling, voter-contact feedback, advertising, and immediate operational pressure.

## Mechanism 2 — possible vendor concealment

The FEC's First General Counsel's Report did not find that the Deep Root theory was factually disproved. It found that:

- the described common-vendor arrangement would constitute an in-kind contribution if implemented;
- graphics in the PAC material appeared to bear Deep Root's logo;
- neither the PAC nor the campaign disclosed direct payments to Deep Root;
- the work might have been planned but never implemented;
- it was also possible the work was disguised by using Deep Root as a subvendor to Fabrizio;
- the PAC paid Fabrizio **$909,450** through May 2, 2022;
- the campaign began paying Fabrizio afterward and paid **$204,450** from June 17 through November 4, 2022;
- the handoff “could raise questions.”

Counsel declined additional investigation because of prosecutorial discretion and the resources required. The Commission therefore did not resolve whether private sharing, subvendor concealment, or successive transfer of the vendor relationship occurred.

This is the strongest concrete covert-operation lead in the election record.

## Mechanism 4 — Rockbridge dark-money and NGO stack

Reuters reported that Vance and Chris Buskirk co-founded Rockbridge in 2019 and that Vance retained informal ties after leaving a formal role. The network uses a private membership model, undisclosed donors, biannual retreats, and a for-profit Delaware donor-management company whose owners are not identified in public corporate records.

Its reported operating stack includes:

| Entity | Function |
|---|---|
| Better Tomorrow | turnout and political operations |
| Over The Horizon Action | turnout and grants |
| The Faithful In Action | church-based political activation |
| Firebrand Action | polling and aligned investigative media |
| Turnout for America | federal independent expenditures and field activity |
| American Moment | personnel identification, training, and placement |

Latest surfaced contribution totals:

- Better Tomorrow: **$7,025,000** in 2024 contributions;
- Over The Horizon Action: **$20,365,119** in 2024 contributions;
- The Faithful In Action: **$2,490,010** in 2024 contributions.

Officer overlap, grants between entities, common vendors, donor-management services, private retreats, and overlap with Vance fundraising can create control without placing the controller's name on the final expenditure or official contact.

This is a plausible intermediary layer for covert influence. Its beneficial ownership and instruction channels remain unresolved.

## Mechanism 5 — personnel placement

American Moment openly presents itself as a pipeline that identifies, educates, credentials, and places personnel into government and policy roles. Vance was a founding-board adviser.

Personnel placement matters because durable control does not require daily orders when aligned staff can select information, draft policy, manage appointments, control procurement, preserve private access, and implement network priorities.

The pipeline is documented. A Thiel-controlled command hierarchy inside it is not yet proved.

## Mechanism 6 — policy and benefit alignment

Vance has used the vice presidency to advocate low-regulation AI development, federal adoption and export of American technology, cryptocurrency-friendly policy, defense-technology procurement, and opposition to incumbent regulatory institutions. Those positions overlap with the economic interests of Thiel, Palantir, Founders Fund, Narya investors, and related defense, AI, crypto, and media companies.

Policy congruence is not enough. Each official act must be matched to:

1. a prior private contact, donor request, retreat discussion, or intermediary tasking;
2. an identifiable beneficiary;
3. a measurable financial, contractual, regulatory, or political benefit;
4. any recusal, waiver, disclosure, or alternative public-interest explanation.

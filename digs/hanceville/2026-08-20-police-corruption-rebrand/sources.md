Retrieved / reviewed: 2026-08-20

## Finding summary

The Hanceville, Alabama police department was dismantled after a corruption investigation involving the former chief and officers, evidence-room failures, and the collapse of felony prosecutions. In July 2026, interim mayor Nolan Bradford said the rebuilt department would be rebranded and that the city would probably change its name in part so future Google searches would not immediately surface the old department's negative history.

The reporting packet supports these specific claims:

- a grand jury recommended abolishing the department and described a rampant culture of corruption;
- five current/former officers and one officer's spouse were indicted;
- an evidence-room audit found more than seven ounces of methamphetamine missing, at least one firearm apparently missing, and roughly 30 undocumented firearms;
- 58 felony cases were dismissed because the grand jury determined cases tied to the former department were unprosecutable after officers' illegal actions;
- the city later dissolved the department and began planning a rebuilt, smaller force;
- Mayor Nolan Bradford said the rebuilt department would be rebranded and floated a name change partly to alter what appears when people search for it online;
- by Aug. 19, 2026, WAFF reported guilty pleas from former Chief Jason Marlin, former officer Jason Wilbanks, Eric Kelso, and Donna Kelso.

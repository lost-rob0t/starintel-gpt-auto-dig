## Established public-source record

Local reporting in 2019 establishes that:

- Mercedes Chico-Sims, 17, was found dead near a pool in Roswell on May 29, 2019.
- The Fulton County Medical Examiner's finding was reported as **homicide**.
- FOX 5 reported that the death certificate described drowning resulting from a physical interaction with another individual.
- WSB-TV reported the death as resulting from **forcible drowning** and said police observed obvious signs of trauma.
- Roswell police arrested Foster and charged him with **concealing the death of another**.
- Contemporary reporting stated that Foster had **not then been charged with murder**.

## Transcript-derived facts and inconsistencies

The operator-supplied YouTube caption transcript is useful evidence but is not treated as a certified interrogation transcript. It depicts Foster materially changing his account over the course of questioning.

The transcript contains or describes:

1. initial denials that Foster was present during the fatal event;
2. later admissions that he was at the pool, entered the water, pulled Mercedes out, and attempted CPR;
3. references to blood / bleeding around the event;
4. movement and concealment of Mercedes' body behind the gazebo;
5. disposal of Mercedes' phone in nearby woods, according to the video's narration;
6. repeated attempts to place responsibility on another person before Foster's account changed.

These facts make a simple “nothing but an accidental drowning” narrative difficult to reconcile with the captured record. They do **not**, standing alone, establish exactly how the fatal physical interaction occurred or prove a specific murder mens rea.

## What does not add up

The unresolved issue is not whether the death was merely classified as suspicious. The captured public record goes further: medical reporting classified it as homicide and tied the drowning to physical interaction with another person. That sits alongside reported trauma, transcript references to blood, Foster's changing account, and concealment behavior.

The current packet therefore preserves two separate propositions:

- **supported:** homicide by drowning involving another person's physical interaction, plus Foster's concealment charge and transcript-described post-event conduct;
- **unresolved:** whether Foster intentionally pushed or held Mercedes underwater, the precise fatal mechanism, and whether prosecutors ever established a homicide offense against him.

Keeping those separate makes the record stronger, not softer. StarIntel should not turn an evidentiary gap into an invented conviction.

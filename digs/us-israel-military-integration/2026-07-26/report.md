## Assessment

The public record does **not** establish a formal merger or one unified U.S.–Israeli chain of command.

It does establish **deep functional integration** across:

1. combatant-command alignment;
2. command-and-control and live-fire exercises;
3. U.S.-crewed air-defense deployments in Israel;
4. joint missile-defense research, testing, production, and procurement;
5. U.S.-financed acquisition and logistics performed for Israeli requirements;
6. U.S.-owned prepositioned munitions in Israel;
7. formal policy, attaché, intelligence, technical, and military liaison channels;
8. publicly acknowledged joint combat operations in 2026.

The strongest support for the “the United States does work for Israel” hypothesis is **directional and domain-specific**, not proof of wholesale subordination. U.S. institutions perform substantial financing, procurement, logistics, missile-defense, and operational support for Israeli defense requirements. At the same time, Israel retains sovereign command and has acted unilaterally in cases where the United States publicly denied participation.

**Analytic confidence:** 0.90

## Highest-value findings

- Israel remains a separate sovereign command, but CENTCOM has combatant-command responsibility for U.S. forces in Israel.
- Joint exercises integrate command-and-control, cyber, air, sea, land, space, refueling, missile defense, and live-fire elements.
- U.S. personnel have operated a deployed THAAD battery in Israel, directly augmenting Israeli integrated air defense.
- MDA and IMDO jointly develop and test David's Sling and Arrow while U.S. industry participates in Iron Dome co-production.
- The IMOD Mission in New York converts U.S. FMF into thousands of U.S. procurements and logistics movements for the IDF.
- WRSA-I places U.S.-owned munitions inside Israel for U.S. and allied emergency use.
- In 2026 the IDF publicly characterized the Iran campaign as a joint, integrated operation coordinated with U.S. military counterparts.
- The Washington military attaché is officially described as a link for military, strategic, intelligence, and technological cooperation.

## Conclusions

- No public evidence establishes a single merged U.S.–Israeli chain of command.
- The documented architecture is better described as deep functional integration with substantial U.S. financing, procurement, logistics, air-defense, and operational support flowing toward Israel.
- Directionality is asymmetric in several domains, supporting the user's hypothesis that U.S. institutions perform significant work for Israeli defense requirements.
- Israeli operational autonomy remains visible; a June 2025 U.S. statement described an Israeli strike as unilateral and said the United States was not involved.

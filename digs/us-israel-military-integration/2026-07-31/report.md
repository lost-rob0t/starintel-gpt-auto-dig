## Executive judgment

The pass-1 conclusion remains intact: the public record does **not** establish one legally merged U.S.–Israeli chain of command.

The evidence now supports a stronger description than a conventional arms relationship:

> **Operational and industrial interdependence with preserved sovereign command.**

The architecture includes publicly described joint combat, U.S.-crewed missile-defense operations alongside the Israeli aerial-defense array, operational-headquarters coordination, Israel-specific engineering performed through the U.S. acquisition system, U.S.-based interceptor production serving both countries, prepositioned U.S. munitions in Israel, and negotiations to make joint investment and co-production central after 2028.

## 1. Joint combat is now the strongest node

The IDF’s March 1, 2026 live-update page states that the IDF and U.S. Armed Forces launched a broad joint operation under the Operation Roaring Lion label. CENTCOM’s Operation Epic Fury page independently states that CENTCOM commenced its campaign at presidential direction.

This establishes coordinated wartime execution more strongly than prior exercise evidence. It does **not** disclose:

- whether either side approved the other’s target packages;
- the combined or parallel nature of operational planning;
- liaison authority;
- intelligence-source division;
- air-tasking-order integration;
- battle-damage-assessment ownership;
- rules for escalation or mission veto.

The packet therefore models the two named campaigns separately and links the IDF campaign to the U.S. Armed Forces through the official joint-operation statement.

## 2. Operational headquarters: verified wording, unresolved structure

The October 2025 DPAG release reports visits to:

- IDF command-and-control centers;
- integrated air-defense systems;
- “bilateral operational headquarters of both nations”;
- the deployed U.S. THAAD battery.

The wording is significant but ambiguous. It may describe:

1. one jointly staffed bilateral headquarters;
2. paired U.S. and Israeli headquarters connected through liaison and shared systems;
3. temporary operation-specific headquarters;
4. a translation artifact describing national headquarters used for bilateral operations.

The graph uses an **unresolved organizational node** rather than inventing a formal name or command authority.

## 3. U.S. personnel operated inside Israel’s combat air-defense architecture

The same DPAG release credits American personnel with operational support during Iranian ballistic-missile intercepts under combat conditions alongside Israel’s aerial-defense array.

This is stronger than equipment transfer. It documents:

- U.S.-crewed equipment;
- U.S. personnel operating in Israel;
- combat-condition interception support;
- functional integration with Israel’s air-defense array.

The source does not establish that U.S. personnel were under Israeli command.

## 4. The regional layer is a federated U.S.-managed defense network

CENTCOM opened the Middle Eastern Air Defense – Combined Defense Operations Cell at Al Udeid Air Base in January 2026. Its public functions include:

- multinational exercise planning;
- drills;
- contingency response;
- information sharing;
- threat warning;
- integrated air- and missile-defense coordination.

CENTCOM’s posture statement describes a broader architecture of combined command posts and U.S. augmentation teams embedded with partner Patriot units.

No official source reviewed in this pass names Israel as a MEAD-CDOC participant. The defensible model is a U.S.-managed regional air-defense architecture that connects to U.S.–Israeli bilateral integration, not a claim that Israel sits inside the Qatar cell.

## 5. Post-2028 negotiations target industrial integration

On June 5, 2026, IMOD announced formal talks for a framework replacing the MOU expiring in 2028.

Named negotiation chain:

| Side | Person | Publicly stated function |
|---|---|---|
| Israel | Amir Baram | Leads Israeli team |
| Israel | Yechiel Leiter | Coordinates closely with Baram |
| United States | Daniel Holler | Leads U.S. team |
| United States | Mike Huckabee | Leads U.S. team |

The stated design is to expand:

- joint investment;
- research and development;
- co-production;
- support for Israel’s qualitative military edge;
- reciprocal partnership.

This does not mean aid has already ended. It is a negotiating objective. The strategic direction, however, is explicit: less visible grant dependence and more deeply coupled production, technology, supply chains, and replenishment.

## 6. The U.S. industrial base now manufactures both sides of the interceptor relationship

RTX states that R2S—a Raytheon-Rafael joint venture—received a $1.25 billion direct-commercial-sales contract for Tamir missiles for Israel.

The East Camden, Arkansas facility:

- was established with $33 million in capital investment;
- produces Tamir for Iron Dome;
- produces SkyHunter for the U.S. Marine Corps’ MRIC program;
- is the first U.S. all-up-round facility for both missile variants.

This creates a concrete interdependence loop:

```text
Israel Missile Defense Organization demand
        ↓
Rafael technology + Raytheon U.S. industrial capacity
        ↓
East Camden production
        ↓
Tamir for Israel + SkyHunter for U.S. Marine Corps
```

## 7. Israel-specific F-35 work runs through U.S. acquisition machinery

Official U.S. contract notices identify Naval Air Systems Command as the contracting activity for Lockheed Martin work under contract `N0001921C0040`.

The 2025 actions include:

- software development;
- systems engineering;
- Israel System Development and Design Phase II;
- `40P02+` weapons certification;
- Block 4 type-vision effectivity capability;
- work concentrated in Fort Worth, Texas;
- noncompetitive modifications funded through Foreign Military Sales customer funds.

The narrow factual claim is not that Israel controls the F-35 program. It is that U.S. military acquisition offices and U.S. contractor engineering teams execute continuing country-specific development for Israel’s F-35 configuration.

## 8. WRSA-I is the logistics and sovereignty hinge

The DoD OIG describes WRSA-I as U.S.-owned missiles, bombs, and other munitions maintained in Israel and available to Israel or other allies with U.S. permission. CENTCOM has operational oversight.

The audit found:

- inconsistent compliance with property-accountability requirements;
- incomplete required inventories during FY2022–FY2024;
- insufficient CENTCOM guidance and monitoring;
- limited audit verification because investigators did not travel to Israel;
- ten recommendations for program controls.

This supports a verified claim of deficient accountability controls. It does **not** prove unauthorized access, loss, diversion, or Israeli ownership.

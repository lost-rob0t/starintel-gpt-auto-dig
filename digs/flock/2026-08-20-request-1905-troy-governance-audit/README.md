# Troy ALPR governance: policy is not configuration

**Request:** #1905  
**Dataset root:** `digs/flock/`  
**Date:** 2026-08-20

Troy's public record now supports a cleaner chronology, but not the happy little fiction that announcing a safeguard proves the platform obeyed it.

## What moved

On May 19, the City of Troy announced a 60-day working period and said annual ALPR audits would be delivered to the mayor and City Council. The same city release said National Lookup would remain disabled except for individualized violent-felony authorizations by the Police Chief, and prohibited immigration-enforcement use and monitoring of First Amendment activity.

The City Council Agenda Center now lists Local Law No. 4 hearings on July 23 and a third hearing on August 6. That proves the legislative process advanced. It does **not** prove enactment, mayoral action, override, or codification.

Recent Times Union reporting adds a concrete implementation dispute. Councilmember Noreen McKee said police supplied full information for only 30 of 81 relevant cases during the 60-day review. The police report contained 826 searches. Mayor Carmella Mantello and Police Chief Daniel DeWolf disputed the council's characterization and said the request had been satisfied.

That disagreement belongs in the record as a disagreement.

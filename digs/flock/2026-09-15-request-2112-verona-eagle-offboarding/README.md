## Verona, Wisconsin

The City of Verona's public November 2025 record identifies its Flock contract and a stated 2026 cost of $9,600; the City's published recap records the Common Council decision not to renew.

Subsequent reporting says the contract lapsed in December 2025 while three cameras remained physically installed into early February 2026. City officials repeatedly asked for removal and covered the cameras while waiting. 404 Media's reporting from the city email production says Flock objected to city staff removing the equipment themselves and that a Flock employee expressed uncertainty about remote disablement.

Those facts establish a defensible lifecycle:

`non-renewal → contract lapse → repeated removal requests → physical covering → later hardware removal`

They do **not** by themselves establish continued collection, account availability, credential state, sharing state, historical-data retention/deletion, or final billing closeout.

## Eagle Sports Range, Cudahy

Multiple Milwaukee-area outlets report that Eagle Sports Range had three Flock cameras around its Cudahy parking lot, requested removal in February 2026, was told contractual obligations prevented immediate removal, and ultimately removed the cameras itself in early August 2026. WISN reported the removed cameras were stored on-site afterward.

This supports:

`customer removal request → reported contract dispute → customer self-removal → hardware custody`

It does **not** establish the governing contract clause, Flock's complete written position, backend account disablement, credential/share state, historical-data deletion, final charges, or any post-removal query activity.

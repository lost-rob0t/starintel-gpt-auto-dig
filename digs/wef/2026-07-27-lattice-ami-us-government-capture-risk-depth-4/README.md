## Strongest new finding

AMI directly participated in a U.S. federal cybersecurity-framework development process. Its October 2023 NIST comment recommended expanding treatment of software and firmware beyond `PROTECT` into `DETECT` and `RECOVER`, arguing that resilience requires technology capable of all three functions.

This is concrete vendor input into federal standards development and aligns with AMI’s firmware-resilience business. It is not proof that NIST adopted the language because of AMI or that the process was captured.

## Federal lobbying

Official LDA filings show Clark Street Associates reported:

- **$40,000 for 2024 Q4**, addressing federal onshore-semiconductor manufacturing funding and FY2025 Department of Defense appropriations.
- **$40,000 for 2025 Q3**, addressing onshore funding and FY2026 Department of Defense appropriations before the House, Senate and Department of Commerce.

House records also list Holland & Knight for Clark Street on behalf of Lattice. The LDA registrant lookup separately lists FGS Global for Lattice, but this pass did not resolve the underlying activity, dates or policy issues.

## Government dependency and protection

Treasury stated in 2017 that:

- the U.S. government uses Lattice products;
- semiconductor supply-chain integrity was important to the government; and
- CFIUS recommended blocking the Canyon Bridge acquisition because the risk could not be mitigated.

That establishes government-customer and national-security-protection relationships. It does not establish improper favoritism.

## Revolving-door and policy channel

Current Lattice CEO Ford Tamer serves on SIA’s board. SIA President and CEO John Neuffer previously spent more than seven years at USTR in deputy assistant USTR roles. SIA credits his tenure with major semiconductor manufacturing-incentive and federal-R&D policy gains.

This is a documented industry–government personnel and advocacy channel. It remains insufficient, without a preferential outcome tied to the relationship, to prove regulatory capture.

## Adjacent federal-benefit branch

Commerce issued Coherent a June 2026 letter of intent for up to **$50 million** in CHIPS direct funding. Coherent is connected to this investigation through former Lattice executives and WEF institutional membership. The funding is recorded only as adjacent context and is not attributed to Lattice or AMI.

## Capture assessment

**Confirmed:**

- recurring lobbying for federal funding and defense appropriations;
- multi-firm lobbying registrations;
- executive access through SIA;
- USTR-to-industry revolving-door movement;
- federal product use and CFIUS protection;
- AMI participation in NIST framework development.

**Not established:**

- vendor-authored text adopted into binding regulation;
- a direct Lattice or AMI federal award;
- tailored procurement rules excluding competitors;
- quid pro quo or campaign-finance exchange;
- a regulator moving directly into Lattice or AMI leadership;
- causal proof that AMI changed the final NIST framework;
- WEF direction of U.S. policy.

The result is **influence architecture confirmed; regulatory capture not yet proven**.

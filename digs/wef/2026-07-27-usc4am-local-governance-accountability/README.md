## Strongest current people

### David T. Woodward

Woodward currently chairs the Oakland County Board of Commissioners while also serving as a public-sector member of the U.S. Center for Advanced Manufacturing executive committee. The centre says that committee sets strategic direction and guides key decisions.

### Dom Holmes

Holmes currently directs Oakland County Economic Development. Before joining county government, he spent more than five years at Automation Alley and led early-stage investment initiatives for advanced-manufacturing companies.

### Sean Carlson

Carlson is Oakland County Deputy County Executive for Economic Development, Information Technology, and Management & Budget. He is also listed on the county Workforce Development Board.

### Stacey Weismiller

The centre's current public team page lists Weismiller as interim director, establishing an operational leadership transition from the earlier Cynthia Hutchison period.

## Historical governance bridge

The centre's 2022 executive-committee announcement named Oakland County Executive David Coulter and MEDC executive Josh Hundt as special advisers. The current committee page lists Woodward and WEF Managing Director Kiva Allgood as public-sector members.

## Completed public outputs

The centre publicly documents:

- the **2024 State of US Manufacturing Report**;
- an innovation playbook developed with the World Economic Forum, Purdue University and Accenture;
- interviews and roundtables involving more than 30 manufacturing leaders.

These are visible outputs, but their funding source and relationship to the measurable outcomes in Michigan Strategic Fund Resolution 2022-080 remain unresolved.

## Attributed accountability findings

A WXYZ investigation reported that public expense records showed:

- `$46,394.82` in Sean Carlson travel, mileage and meal expenses from May 2022 through August 2025;
- a May 2023 Milwaukee trip with David Woodward, billed at `$2,186.84`, described as a USC4AM conference but lacking conference documentation in the receipts reviewed;
- an Austin trip billed at more than `$2,767.85`, labeled as a conference but also lacking conference documentation in the reviewed records;
- a later `$495` repayment for mileage overpayments, according to Oakland County's response.

The county told WXYZ that the travel was budgeted relationship-building work that helped secure more than `$1.3 billion` in investment and 4,500 jobs.

These findings remain **attributed secondary reporting**. The underlying expense reports, approvals, receipts, conference agendas and attendee records were not independently obtained in this pass. A documentation gap does not establish an improper trip, personal benefit, fraud or corruption.

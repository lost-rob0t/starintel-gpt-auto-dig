## Bottom line

Graphika generally does **not** possess the platform-side authority to ban an account or delete a post. Its documented role is upstream: identify actors and narratives, map spread, submit or support tickets and flags, brief platforms and government partners, and recommend enforcement or counter-messaging. Platforms then apply labels, downranking, sharing restrictions, removals, suspensions, or bans.

The evidence supports calling Graphika part of a **moderation and information-control pipeline**. It does not support rewriting every downstream platform action as a unilateral Graphika decision.

## Causal scale

| Level | Meaning | Standard |
|---:|---|---|
| 3 | Direct report-to-enforcement | A platform explicitly says it banned or removed accounts referenced or flagged in a Graphika report. |
| 2 | Coalition flag-to-action | Graphika is a named coalition member or coauthor; the coalition flags or reports content and later documents platform action, but individual attribution is unavailable. |
| 1 | Targeting or enforcement advocacy | A Graphika-linked coalition names accounts/outlets or recommends labels, probation, sanctions, removal, or account/community-level enforcement without proving a resulting action. |

## Documented moderation cases

### 1. Spamouflage accounts banned after Graphika report — Level 3

Graphika identified 15 X accounts, one TikTok account, and a cross-platform fake media persona posing as U.S. voters or activists ahead of the 2024 election. TikTok subsequently stated that the accounts referenced in Graphika's report had been banned. Related YouTube and Instagram assets had also been taken down.

This is the cleanest direct sequence found:

`Graphika identifies accounts → public report → platform confirms referenced accounts banned`

**Boundary:** These were deceptive personas attributed to a foreign influence operation, not disclosed domestic political speakers.

Sources:

- Graphika, *The #Americans*
- NPR, *China is pushing divisive political messages online using fake U.S. voters*

### 2. Absentee-ballot request-form posts labeled or taken down — Level 2

The Election Integrity Partnership investigated and reported the cross-platform spread of misleading claims about absentee-ballot request forms. Its incident report says Facebook and TikTok applied labels or takedowns, while the highest-traction tweet remained unactioned.

Graphika was one of the EIP's four core organizations. The public incident record does not identify which partner submitted each item, so attribution to Graphika alone would be false.

### 3. Graphika-coauthored Virality Project flag followed by Instagram removal — Level 2

A Virality Project report coauthored by Graphika analysts Kyle Weiss and Erin McAweeney says project analysts flagged an Instagram video promoting a vaccine-shedding narrative. The video received more than 360,000 views and had been removed by Instagram when the report was published. The same analysis recorded warning labels on related posts.

The sequence is documented, but Instagram did not publicly state that the project flag caused the removal.

### 4. Removed VAERS TikTok video and named vaccine critics — Level 1

A Virality Project article coauthored by Graphika analyst Erin McAweeney noted that a TikTok video teaching users to search VAERS had been removed. It also identified Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Joseph Mercola, and Children's Health Defense as recurring sources in the monitored network.

This establishes monitoring, public profiling, and awareness of removal. It does **not** establish that Graphika or the Virality Project caused the TikTok removal or caused enforcement against the named people and organization.

### 5. EIP named domestic “repeat offenders” and recommended stronger sanctions — Level 1

The EIP publicly categorized Gateway Pundit, Breitbart, Just the News, and Breitbart editor Josh Caplan as repeat spreaders. It recommended stronger platform sanctions, including repeat-offender labels and potentially removal. The EIP final report separately recommended probationary status, monitoring, and sanctions for repeat policy violations.

This is direct evidence of enforcement advocacy aimed at identifiable domestic outlets and speakers. It is not proof that the recommendations produced a particular ban.

### 6. Virality Project advocated account- and community-level moderation — Level 1

A Virality Project analysis argued that content-level removals were insufficient and recommended that platforms target the accounts and communities using moderation-avoidance strategies. It acknowledged that such techniques can also protect politically controversial but socially responsible speech.

The specific article was authored by University of Washington researchers, so it is recorded as coalition-level policy advocacy rather than an individually authored Graphika recommendation.

## What “censored by Graphika” can honestly mean

**Supported wording:**

- Graphika supplied intelligence that platforms used or could use for enforcement.
- Graphika participated in ticketing and flagging coalitions whose work was followed by labels and takedowns.
- Graphika-linked coalitions recommended sanctions against repeat domestic speakers and broader account/community enforcement.
- A platform explicitly banned deceptive accounts referenced in a Graphika report.

**Not supported without more evidence:**

- Graphika personally pressed the delete or ban control.
- Every EIP or Virality Project platform action resulted from Graphika's own submission.
- Every person publicly profiled by a Graphika-linked project was later censored because of that profiling.
- A court has made a merits finding that Graphika unlawfully censored a named plaintiff.

## Operational synthesis

The awards form a coherent progression:

1. map communities and high-impact narratives;
2. forecast virality and critical mass;
3. measure engagement with target audiences;
4. infer causal links between online narratives and riots, voting, or other offline action;
5. identify community-specific language and calls to action for MISO planners;
6. detect coordinated or AI-driven accounts;
7. geographically pinpoint influence;
8. design counter-messaging and maintain information advantage;
9. provide persistent information-battlespace awareness to combatant commands.

The censorship concern is not that every capability is inherently censorship. The concern is the end-to-end integration: **surveillance → classification → targeting → recommendation → platform or government intervention → effectiveness measurement**.

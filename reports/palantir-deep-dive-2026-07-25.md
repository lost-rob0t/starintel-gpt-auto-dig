## Executive assessment

Palantir is no longer merely a government analytics vendor. It is an operational software layer embedded across defense, intelligence, immigration, health, revenue administration and commercial decision systems. Its leverage comes from four things operating together:

1. **Platform depth:** Gotham, Foundry, Apollo and AIP connect data, permissions, models and operational workflows.
2. **Procurement scale:** the company has moved from individual programs toward enterprise agreements, blanket purchase agreements and long-duration platform contracts.
3. **Influence capacity:** Palantir combines a large in-house federal lobbying operation with a bipartisan network of defense, appropriations, health, aviation and executive-branch specialists.
4. **Governance concentration:** the Founder Voting Trust and Class F structure preserve unusual founder voting power.

The main analytical error to avoid is treating every headline contract number as cash received. The Army's **$10 billion** figure is a not-to-exceed ceiling, Maven's **$795 million** modification is funded through orders, and Palantir's 2025 10-K explicitly excludes **$12.3 billion** of unfunded IDIQ ceiling from remaining deal value.

## Structural findings

### 1. Enterprise-contract migration

The Army agreement's consolidation of 75 contracts is strategically more important than the $10 billion headline. It lowers transaction friction, centralizes purchasing and makes Palantir easier to expand across commands and use cases.

### 2. Lobbying and program alignment

Palantir's filings do not merely list generic "technology" policy. They identify programs and procurement domains closely adjacent to active business: TITAN, command-and-control, Battlefield Domain Awareness, JADC2, VA modernization, FAA/ATC, public health data platforms and federal software procurement.

This establishes **alignment**, not causation. The next step is a dated sequence of lobbying issue text, appropriations language, solicitations, sole-source justifications, task orders and modifications.

### 3. Platform lock-in

GAO protest history shows recurring disputes over commercial-item preference, brand-name Palantir requirements and procurement vehicles. Lock-in risk is not only file formats. It can include ontology, permissions, lineage, application logic, user training, integrations and operational dependency.

### 4. Data-governance exposure

Palantir says it supplies software and customers control their data. Critics characterize the company as a surveillance and weapons actor. The defensible analytical position is narrower: Palantir's platforms can integrate highly sensitive data and operational workflows, while the exact source systems, permissions, retention rules and user actions vary by deployment and are often incompletely public.

### 5. Founder control

The Founder Voting Trust consists of Alex Karp, Stephen Cohen and Peter Thiel. The 2026 proxy describes Class F mechanics capable of preserving founder voting power near 49.999999% under specified circumstances. This is a material governance-concentration risk.

### 6. International public-sector expansion

The UK is the clearest non-U.S. node: a health-data platform with a maximum value of £330 million and a £240.6 million ex-VAT Ministry of Defence enterprise agreement. Palantir also maintains operating subsidiaries across Europe, Asia-Pacific, Israel, Qatar and the Americas.

## Highest-value follow-up investigations

1. **Contract ledger:** build award → modification → task order → obligation → outlay → revenue mappings.
2. **Lobbying chronology:** diff every amended LD-2 and map issue text to procurement milestones.
3. **Data-flow registry:** identify source systems, data classes, controllers, processors, retention and audit controls for each deployment.
4. **Lock-in audit:** document exportability, ontology portability, replacement costs and termination-assistance clauses.
5. **Personnel graph:** expand covered positions, committees, agencies, dates and clients for every registered lobbyist.
6. **Foreign public-sector graph:** add ministerial meetings, tender evaluators, subcontractors and local Palantir entities.
7. **Oversight corpus:** ingest inspector-general reports, privacy impact assessments, court filings, FOIA releases and bid protests.

## Source hygiene

- Contract **ceiling**, **potential value**, **current award amount**, **obligation**, **outlay** and **recognized revenue** are separate fields.
- Latest amended lobbying filings supersede original reports.
- Subcontractor registrations are not automatically additional spend.
- Former government service is an influence-network indicator, not proof of corruption.
- Corporate claims and advocacy claims retain their source labels.
- Classified scope and confidential commercial contracts create unavoidable visibility gaps.

# Deep Auto-Dig: Beth Din / Religious Court Corruption

## Mission

Dig hard into documented corruption, abuse, coercion, conflicts of interest, financial misconduct, procedural misconduct, favoritism, retaliation, cover-ups, and accountability failures involving religious courts, with **Beth Din / batei din as the primary focus**.

This is not a race, ethnicity, or anti-religion investigation. Do not generalize conduct by individuals or institutions to Jews, Jewish communities, or religious people as a class. Follow the institutions, decisions, money, relationships, complaints, court records, disciplinary records, and receipts.

## Primary target

Beth Din institutions and actors involved in adjudication, arbitration, divorce/get proceedings, conversion disputes, communal discipline, commercial disputes, family disputes, or other quasi-judicial functions.

Prioritize cases where there is evidence of:

- bribery, kickbacks, improper payments, undisclosed fees, or financial self-dealing;
- conflicts of interest, undisclosed relationships, repeat-player favoritism, or insider dealing;
- coercion, threats, retaliation, blacklisting, social pressure, extortionate conduct, or abuse of communal leverage;
- manipulation or abuse of get/divorce proceedings;
- procedural unfairness, ex parte contacts, denial of notice, inability to present evidence, inconsistent rules, or sham hearings;
- fabricated, altered, concealed, or selectively presented evidence;
- pressure on witnesses, complainants, litigants, attorneys, rabbis, employers, schools, synagogues, or community organizations;
- cover-ups, suppression of complaints, intimidation of whistleblowers, or retaliation against critics;
- criminal conduct by judges/dayanim, administrators, associated fixers, or intermediaries;
- civil judgments, arbitration-vacatur proceedings, sanctions, contempt findings, injunctions, disciplinary actions, or appellate criticism tied to Beth Din conduct;
- patterns showing one institution or actor repeatedly accused across independent sources;
- governance failures: opaque appointment systems, no meaningful appeal, financial opacity, lack of recusal rules, lack of published procedure, lack of complaint mechanisms, or conflicts between religious authority and private arbitration power.

## Comparative secondary scope

After the Beth Din pass, enumerate comparable documented corruption or abuse involving other religious or faith-based courts and tribunals. Use these only to identify recurring governance patterns and useful comparison points. Do not dilute the Beth Din focus.

Relevant comparison categories can include:

- Islamic/Sharia councils or tribunals;
- Christian ecclesiastical courts or church tribunals;
- canon-law tribunals;
- other faith-based arbitration bodies.

Again: investigate institutions and conduct, not ethnic or religious populations.

## Research method

Go deeper than media summaries. Build every significant finding from the strongest available evidence.

Evidence priority:

1. court opinions, dockets, filings, judgments, injunctions, sanctions, arbitration-vacatur decisions, and appellate opinions;
2. criminal indictments, plea agreements, convictions, sentencing documents, law-enforcement releases, and regulator records;
3. bar, judicial, rabbinical, professional, charity, corporate, and nonprofit disciplinary or registry records;
4. official Beth Din rules, fee schedules, arbitration agreements, recusal rules, published decisions, governance documents, and archived versions of those materials;
5. sworn declarations, deposition excerpts, exhibits, contemporaneous correspondence, recordings where lawfully public, and authenticated documentary evidence;
6. high-quality investigative reporting with named sources and primary-document links;
7. first-person allegations and community reporting only as leads until independently corroborated.

Do not turn allegations into facts. Label every claim as one of:

- **proven/adjudicated** — established by conviction, judgment, sanction, disciplinary finding, or equivalent authoritative result;
- **documented** — strongly supported by primary records but not finally adjudicated;
- **alleged** — claimed by a party/source and not independently established;
- **disputed** — materially contested by credible evidence or a named opposing party;
- **unverified lead** — worth further digging but presently unsupported.

## Entity enumeration

Enumerate, normalize, and link:

- Beth Din / tribunal name and aliases;
- jurisdiction/location;
- denomination or institutional affiliation when relevant;
- judges/dayanim;
- administrators and staff;
- attorneys and repeat representatives;
- parties to significant proceedings;
- intermediaries, fixers, advocates, communal organizations, schools, synagogues, charities, businesses, or employers materially involved in a documented event;
- parent organizations and funding/governance relationships;
- civil courts reviewing or enforcing the tribunal's decisions;
- regulators, prosecutors, disciplinary bodies, and appellate courts involved.

For people, only collect personal data that is relevant to the documented public-interest investigation. Do not dox private individuals or collect unrelated family/address data.

## Case record schema

For every substantive case/event, record:

- stable case ID;
- tribunal/institution;
- people and organizations involved;
- location and jurisdiction;
- date range;
- proceeding type;
- alleged misconduct category;
- precise factual claim;
- claim status: proven/documented/alleged/disputed/unverified;
- strongest primary source;
- supporting sources;
- response/denial from accused party if available;
- court or disciplinary outcome;
- sanctions/remedies;
- financial amounts where documented;
- related cases/events;
- confidence score;
- remaining unanswered questions.

## Pattern analysis

Once cases are enumerated, test for actual patterns instead of vibes:

- repeated actors across nominally separate tribunals;
- repeat litigants or counsel receiving unusual treatment;
- recurring conflicts or undisclosed ties;
- unusual fee flows;
- clusters of vacated arbitration awards;
- repeated coercion or retaliation allegations from independent complainants;
- procedural rules applied inconsistently;
- institutions with no meaningful recusal/appeal/complaint mechanism;
- civil courts repeatedly criticizing a tribunal or the same individuals;
- organizations routing disputes toward a tribunal in which they have governance or financial influence;
- chronology showing complaints preceding later proven misconduct.

Do not claim systemic corruption from one case. State exactly what the evidence supports and what it does not.

## Output

Produce a deep research dossier, not an opinion piece.

Required sections:

1. **Executive findings** — strongest verified findings only.
2. **Beth Din institution map** — which tribunals appear in the evidence and how they relate.
3. **Case ledger** — every meaningful case/event with status and citations.
4. **Actor ledger** — recurring judges, administrators, counsel, organizations, and intermediaries.
5. **Proven misconduct** — adjudicated findings only.
6. **Credible documented allegations** — clearly separated from proven misconduct.
7. **Procedural and governance weaknesses** — rules and structures that create corruption risk.
8. **Money / conflicts map** — documented financial relationships and conflicts.
9. **Civil-court review** — enforcement, vacatur, sanctions, criticism, appeals.
10. **Comparative religious-court cases** — only after the Beth Din core is complete.
11. **Contradictory evidence and defenses** — include serious rebuttals; do not bury them.
12. **Open questions / next dig targets**.
13. **Source table** — URL/document, source type, date, primary/secondary, reliability notes.

## Hard rules

- Facts first. Receipts first. No race angle.
- Do not treat Judaism, Jews, Orthodox Jews, rabbis, or religious communities as collectively responsible for misconduct by specific courts or actors.
- Do not inflate allegations into findings.
- Do not use anonymous internet accusations as evidence unless they lead to corroborating records.
- Do not pad the dossier with generic criticism of religion.
- Include exculpatory evidence and corrections when found.
- Distinguish private arbitration authority from state judicial authority.
- Flag jurisdictional differences: a Beth Din in New York is not automatically comparable to one in London, Israel, Canada, Australia, etc.
- Where a tribunal name is generic, resolve the exact institution before merging records.
- Preserve provenance for every material assertion.
- Prefer primary documents even when they make the story less dramatic.

## Stop condition

The task is complete when the research has enumerated the major publicly documented Beth Din corruption/misconduct cases discoverable across multiple jurisdictions, resolved the principal recurring actors and institutions, separated proven conduct from allegations, identified governance patterns supported by multiple independent cases, and produced a source-complete dossier with clear unresolved leads.
